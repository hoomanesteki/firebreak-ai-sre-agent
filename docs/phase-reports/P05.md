# Phase 05: Eval harness core

## Summary

The eval harness works end to end: `firebreak eval run --config b0 --split
test_ood --trials 3` investigates every task in a split, grades each trial
against its label, and writes JSON and Markdown with bootstrap intervals,
calibration, selective accuracy and pass^3.

**The headline result corrects Phase 4.** That report led with "B0 scores 59 of
59, so the eval design has no headroom". That was true of train and validation,
which the thresholds were tuned against, and false of the held out splits:

| Split | Top-1 | 95% interval | pass^3 | Coverage | ECE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| validation (tuned against) | 100.0% | [100.0%, 100.0%] | 100.0% | 80% | 0.5334 |
| test_id (held out) | 90.3% | [80.6%, 100.0%] | 90.3% | 84% | 0.4171 |
| **test_ood (families held out)** | **80.0%** | **[63.3%, 93.3%]** | **80.0%** | 100% | 0.1383 |

There is headroom, and it is where it should be: twenty points of it on fault
families the system has never been tuned on. Phase 4's conclusion was drawn
from the only data available at the time and should have been stated as being
about that data.

## What was built

- **`evals/statistics.py`**: bootstrap and paired bootstrap intervals, Brier
  score, expected and maximum calibration error with the reliability diagram,
  selective accuracy with a risk-coverage curve, pass@k and pass^k.
- **`evals/outcome.py`**: `InvestigationOutcome`, the one shape every
  configuration produces.
- **`evals/graders.py`**: the six code graders from SPEC.md Section 9.2.
- **`evals/metrics.py`**: aggregation, per family and per split.
- **`evals/runner.py`**: `run_configuration`, and B0 wired in as a
  configuration.
- **`evals/report.py`**: JSON and Markdown from one computation.
- **`evals/gate.py`** and **`config/eval_gate.yaml`**: the non-inferiority
  gate and its margins.
- **`cli/eval.py`**: `firebreak eval run`.
- A confidence for B0, so there is something to calibrate.

## Where the headroom is

The per-family breakdown is the useful part, and it says something specific.

| Split | Family | Top-1 | Top-3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| test_id | error-injection | 62% (5 of 8) | 100% |
| test_id | everything else | 100% | 100% |
| test_ood | resource | 100% (24 of 24) | 100% |
| **test_ood** | **contention** | **0% (0 of 6)** | **100%** |

**Top-3 is 100% on every split and every family.** The culprit is always among
the suspects. What fails is the ordering, and on the contention family it fails
every single time.

That distinction is why SPEC.md Section 9.2 asks for both graders, and it
changes what Phase 6 should attack: an agent does not need to find something
the deterministic pipeline missed, it needs to reorder three candidates the
pipeline already produced. That is a much more tractable problem than root
cause discovery, and it is a good thing to learn before building the agent
rather than after.

**No threshold was touched in response to any of this.** Tuning on a held out
split would spend the only thing that makes these numbers worth having.
`docs/adr` and `config/thresholds.yaml` are unchanged by this phase. Any fix
for the contention ordering has to be developed against train and validation
and only then measured here.

## Calibration

B0 states a confidence derived from the ratio between its first and second
candidate, because a configuration that states none cannot be calibrated at
all and the whole calibration column would be empty for the baseline.

It is poorly calibrated, and the numbers show why in a way worth recording:

| Split | Accuracy | Typical confidence | ECE |
|---|---|---|---|
| validation | 100% | about 0.55 | 0.5334 |
| test_id | 90.3% | about 0.55 | 0.4171 |
| test_ood | 80.0% | about 0.55 | 0.1383 |

The error falls as accuracy falls, which is not the system improving. It is the
system's fixed confidence becoming accidentally closer to a lower accuracy.
**Calibration cannot be measured on saturated data**: when accuracy is 100%,
any confidence below 1.0 is underconfident by construction, so validation's
0.53 says nothing about the confidence function and everything about the split.

The margin ratio is a reasonable ordering of confidence and it is not a
probability. It is labelled as such in `pipeline.py` and it should be
recalibrated against recorded incidents before any report quotes it.

## The eval gate

Non-inferiority on the lower bound of a paired bootstrap difference, with the
margins in `config/eval_gate.yaml`.

Building it surfaced a real constraint. On a thirty task split a single flipped
task moves the point estimate by 3.3 points and drags the bootstrap lower bound
to about minus ten. SPEC.md Section 9.4's default three point margin is
therefore narrower than the split can resolve, and a gate that reported
`FAIL_QUALITY` there would be failing on noise and teaching people to re-run it
until it passed.

The gate now separates two causes of a failed non-inferiority test:

- the point estimate is past the margin, so a regression was measured:
  `FAIL_QUALITY`;
- the point estimate is inside the margin but the interval is not, so nothing
  was observed and nothing was excluded: `INCONCLUSIVE`.

That is the textbook treatment of an underpowered non-inferiority test, it
keeps the margin at the value SPEC.md specifies, and it makes the real
constraint visible: **the library needs to be larger, not the margin looser.**
A 31 task split cannot resolve a 3 point difference, and no amount of
statistics will change that.

## Defects found

1. **The bootstrap resampled trials instead of tasks.** This one shipped, and
   my own docstring had already said not to do it: `bootstrap_mean` documents
   the task as the resampling unit and `compute_metrics` passed one observation
   per trial. Running three trials of B0, which is deterministic and therefore
   produces three identical trials, narrowed the reported interval on test_id
   from [77.4, 100.0] to [83.9, 95.7]. The extra confidence came from nowhere.
   A test now asserts that extra trials of a deterministic configuration leave
   the interval untouched.
2. **`evaluate_gate` took four positional lists**, grouping both sides' sheets
   and then both sides' outcomes. The natural reading is "candidate, then
   baseline", so the first caller transposed them, and a transposed comparison
   produces a verdict about nothing while looking entirely normal. Replaced
   with one `Side` object per configuration, which makes the mistake
   impossible rather than documented.
3. **pass@k and pass^k disagreed at k=1**, where they are the same quantity.
   `1 - C(4,1)/C(5,1)` is 0.19999999999999996 and `C(1,1)/C(5,1)` is 0.2. Both
   now compute exactly and convert once, because a metric that is off in the
   last bits cannot be compared between runs by equality.
4. **A grader with no applicable trials reported zero accuracy**, which reads
   as "always wrong" where the truth is "never asked". Now `None`, with
   coverage beside it.
5. **I wrote a `_unused_datetime_guard` function** whose only purpose was to
   justify an import I did not need. Deleted along with the import.
6. **Three commit-message rejections**, two of them subjects over 72
   characters and one an undeclared scope. The gate works. What does not work
   is my sequencing: a rejected commit leaves its files staged, and twice now
   the next commit in a chain has swept them in under the wrong message. Both
   were split back apart, and the lesson is not to chain commits.

## Caveats

**Every number here is from synthetic fixtures**, and each report says so in
its own fields and at the top of its Markdown. The `quotable_as_a_result`
field is `false` on all three.

**The test splits have now been read.** That was the point of having them and
it is not free: each reading costs a little of their independence, because I
now know that contention ordering is the weak spot. The discipline from here is
that fixes are developed against train and validation, and the test splits are
read again only for a release candidate.

**A 31 task split is too small for the gate's own margins.** Recorded incidents
would not change that by themselves; the library needs more scenarios per
family as well.

## Known issues

**Two graders are not built.** The model-based mechanism judge and the human
usefulness rubric both need a model and owner labels. SPEC.md Section 9.2 wants
the judge calibrated against 60 owner-labelled reports with Cohen's kappa
reported before its output is trusted, and that is a Phase 8 task.

**Three metrics have no data yet**, because B0 makes no claim they could score:
fault class, onset error, and remediation. The graders exist and are tested on
hand-made cases; they report "not claimed" rather than zero, which is the
honest state.

**No incident library is recorded.** Still the critical path, and now with a
sharper reason: the harness is ready and will produce a real number the day
there is real data.

**Trajectory health is not collected.** SPEC.md Section 9.3 metric 7 asks for
repeated tool calls, stalls and budget stops, and none of those exist until
there is an agent in Phase 6.

## Open questions for the owner
