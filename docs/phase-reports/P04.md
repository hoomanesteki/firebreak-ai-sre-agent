# Phase 04: Knowledge graph and deterministic triage

## Summary

Deterministic triage is built end to end: a bundle goes in, a ranked list of
suspects comes out with evidence attached, and a written report comes out the
other side with no model involved. The knowledge graph is loaded into Neo4j
and the Cypher was verified against a running database rather than assumed.

The single most important result of this phase is uncomfortable, so it goes
first. **B0 scores 59 of 59 on the synthetic fixtures.** Deterministic triage
with no model at all gets every case right, including all ten no-fault cases.

That is not a success. It means the fixtures are too easy, and it means the
eval design currently has no headroom: no agent can beat a baseline that is
already perfect, so every comparison Phase 5 onwards is meant to make is
unmeasurable until the incident library is recorded. Recording it is now the
critical path for the project, not a task waiting its turn.

The second result is that **a published paper's recommendations did not
transfer, and measuring rather than trusting them was worth doing.** Two
mechanisms from eIRWR, adopted on the paper's authority, made the ranking
worse than using no graph at all.

## What was built

- **`graph/ranking.py`**: personalised PageRank over the call graph, with the
  power iteration written out rather than taken from NetworkX.
- **`graph/knowledge.py`**: the hand written knowledge, validated and cross
  referenced on load.
- **`graph/schema.cypher`, `graph/queries.py`, `graph/load.py`**: the Neo4j
  schema, thirteen named parameterised queries, and an idempotent loader.
- **`triage/scoring.py`**: the anomaly scorer, declared once and shared.
- **`triage/pipeline.py`**: bundle to ranked candidates, plus abstention.
- **`triage/thresholds.py`** and **`config/thresholds.yaml`**: every tuned
  number with the measurement that justified it.
- **`triage/report.py`**: baseline B0, a written report with citations.
- **`tools/runbooks.py`**: `runbook_search`, the twelfth of fourteen tools.
- **`knowledge/`**: 16 services, 6 teams, 3 allowed actions, 21 runbooks.
- **`ops/compose.core.yml`**: Neo4j 5.26.1, pinned.
- Three measurement scripts, and ADR-0006 and ADR-0007.

## What the graph does, measured

From `reports/triage/ranking_ablation.json`, 147 trials over the train and
validation splits. Regenerate with `make measure-ranking`.

| Configuration | Culprit ranked first | Mean reciprocal rank |
|---|---|---|
| anomaly scoring only, no graph | 92 of 147 | 0.813 |
| **as shipped** | **147 of 147** | **1.000** |
| without endpoint scaling | 143 | 0.986 |
| with self-loops | 135 | 0.950 |
| with backward edges | 10 | 0.534 |
| eIRWR as published | 56 | 0.691 |
| restart vector sharpened, exponent 2 | 143 | 0.986 |
| restart vector sharpened, exponent 3 | 141 | 0.980 |

The Phase 3 target was to move the culprit from 60 percent at rank one toward
the 100 percent it already reached at rank three. On these fixtures that is
met. See the caveats section for why that sentence should not be read as an
achievement.

**The interesting row is `with backward edges`.** eIRWR adds backward edges
at a ratio of 0.3 and reports them as an improvement. Here they take top-1
from 147 to 10, far below the 92 that no graph at all achieves, because they
feed score straight back into the upstream symptoms the ranking exists to
demote. Self-loops cost 12 trials for the same underlying reason.

Both are kept in the code, defaulting to off, with the measured cost recorded
beside each constant. Deleting them would make the decision unfalsifiable,
and it is a decision that should be re-examined on recorded data.

## Abstention, which the no-fault family exists to test

A ranking cannot say "nothing is wrong". PageRank returns an order whatever
it is given, so a healthy system produces a confident looking first place,
and reporting it is how an operator learns to ignore the tool.

Separating the cases needs a magnitude rather than an order. Over 147 faulted
and 30 no-fault trials the two populations did not overlap: the loudest quiet
system scored 7.7 and the quietest real incident scored 66.8. The threshold
sits at 22.7, the geometric midpoint of that gap, rather than on either edge.

Choosing the midpoint was a defect fix, not a preference. Every threshold
inside a separating gap scores a perfect Youden J, so the first version broke
that tie on "lowest" and landed exactly on the loudest quiet recording
observed, which is the most fragile point in the range.

**A gap that wide is the clearest evidence in the repository that the
fixtures are easier than reality.** No production telemetry separates a
quiet afternoon from an incident by a factor of nine.

## ADR-0006, decided on a measurement

`reports/triage/log_template_comparison.json`, 1,920 labelled lines.

| | Clusters found (320 true) | Purity | Stable under reordering |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Masker** | 280 | **0.875** | **Yes** |
| Drain | 88 | 0.275 | No |

Restricted to error severities, the only condition `top_error_signatures`
runs in because it filters before clustering:

| | Clusters found (112 true) | Purity |
|---|---|---|
| **Masker** | **112** | **1.000** |
| Drain | 32 | 0.286 |

The decisive property is stability. Reordering the identical corpus moved 180
of Drain's 1,920 lines into different clusters on one shuffle of three and
left the other two untouched. Intermittent is what makes it dangerous: an
evidence id is a hash over a result, and a citation that stops matching two
runs in ten is worse than one that never matches.

Deciding this required fixing the fixtures first, which is recorded below.

## Defects found

1. **The synthetic log bodies had no variable parts.** `mask_log_line`
   changed nothing on any of 293 lines in a bundle, so the masker,
   `top_error_signatures`, and the whole question ADR-0006 exists to settle
   were being tested against data that could not distinguish a working
   implementation from a broken one. This is the Phase 3 pattern in a new
   place: working code, and a fixture that never exercises it.
2. **I wrote the eIRWR mechanisms in on the paper's authority and they were
   harmful.** Caught only because the ablation existed. Had I written them in
   without measuring, the ranking would have shipped at 56 of 147 while the
   report claimed the graph helped.
3. **The abstention threshold sat on the edge of the gap**, the most fragile
   point available, because the tie-break was "lowest" rather than "most
   robust".
4. **The B0 report argued against its own conclusion.** It named whichever
   service sorted first as the likely origin even when every onset was tied
   at zero, which happens in about 30 percent of trials because the window
   opens after the fault has taken hold. Found by reading the output, not by
   a test.
5. **`runbook_search` scoring failed at the job it was written for.** A
   logarithmic dampener let a section repeating one query word forty times
   outrank one covering all four terms, 3.71 against 2.77. Replaced with BM25
   term saturation, which caps each term at 1 and makes breadth win by
   construction. Found by a test written from the docstring's own claim.
6. **`Path.relative_to` raises rather than falling back**, in two places. A
   knowledge directory outside the repository turned a valid runbook into a
   validation error, and a measurement script crashed after the work was done
   purely while describing where the report went.
7. **`edges_from_topology` was the defensive reader I removed in Phase 3.** I
   wrote it, noticed, and replaced it with `EdgeRecord.from_row` so both
   readers share one parse. Tolerating drift is what let the earlier
   instances go unnoticed.
8. **`Edge` was a third shape for a concept `EdgeRecord` already declared.**
   Removed before it shipped.
9. **The OPTIONAL MATCH collect trap.** A service with no datastore reported
   one datastore that does not exist, because a collect over a missing
   optional match returns a list holding one map of nulls. Typechecks
   perfectly; invisible without a database. Confirmed empirically, not
   assumed.
10. **A duplicated log template in my own fixture.** The warn and healthy GET
    templates were textually identical, so the ground truth labelled one
    template as two and scored both clusterers as merging something they were
    right to merge.
11. **The leakage rule I added flagged twelve runbooks for the word
    "latency".** The rule was wrong, not the runbooks: `latency` is a fault
    class value and also ordinary technical English. Narrowed to the
    underscored tokens, which are the actual vocabulary.
12. **A commit landed under the wrong message.** A rejected commit left its
    files staged and the next commit swept them in, so the Neo4j work sat
    inside a commit about `runbook_search`. Split back apart. My own
    commit-message hook caught two other mistakes in the same session, an
    undeclared scope and a 76 character subject, which is the gate working.

## Caveats, which matter more than the numbers

**Every figure in this report is from synthetic fixtures.** Each report file
says so in its own fields. None of them may be quoted as a result.

**Perfect scores are a statement about the fixtures, not the system.** The
synthetic builder makes the target and its call path symptomatic, and a
forward random walk accumulates at the end of that path, so the fixture and
the algorithm are close to built for each other. 147 of 147 and 59 of 59 are
what that looks like.

**The eval design has no headroom until real data exists.** B0 at 100 percent
leaves nothing for B1, FB, or any ablation to demonstrate. This is now the
binding constraint on the project.

**The onset bonus has never been shown to do anything.** It neither helps nor
hurts, because the fixtures are saturated. It is kept because the reason it
shows nothing is a lack of headroom rather than a demonstration of
uselessness, which is a different claim.

## Known issues

**No incident library is recorded, and this is now the critical path.** Four
things wait on it: the two live backends, the backend parity tests, an honest
B0 figure, and any headroom for Phase 5 to measure anything at all.

**Jaeger and OpenSearch backends are still unwritten**, carried from Phase 3,
along with the collector's failing OpenSearch logs exporter.

**Two of fourteen tools remain.** `similar_incidents` needs Phase 10's
incident memory, `propose_remediation` needs Phase 9's approval service. The
schema and the query for the first are already in place.

**Retrieval is lexical and structured rather than dense.** SPEC.md Section
6.3 asks for hybrid retrieval; embeddings were rejected on determinism, since
an evidence id is a hash over a result. Adding the dense half later needs a
pinned model, committed vectors, and a cross-machine test.
